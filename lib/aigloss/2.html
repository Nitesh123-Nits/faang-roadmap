CHAPTER I

Foundations of the Art

The elementary vocabulary upon which all else in this book is built. Know these first, for every later term presumes them.

No. 1

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

The broad discipline of building systems that perform tasks normally requiring human judgment — perceiving, reasoning, deciding, and generating language.

No. 2

Machine Learning (ML)

A branch of AI wherein a program improves its performance on a task by learning patterns from data, rather than following rules written explicitly by hand.

No. 3

Deep Learning

A subfield of ML using neural networks of many stacked layers to learn increasingly abstract representations of raw data, such as pixels or words.

No. 4

Model

The artifact produced by training — a set of learned parameters plus an architecture, that maps inputs to outputs.

No. 5

Parameters & Weights

The internal numeric values a model adjusts during training. A model's "size" (e.g. 7B, 70B) refers to its parameter count.

ENGINEER’S NOTE

think of parameters the way you'd think of a huge, tuned configuration file that training writes for you rather than one you write by hand.

No. 6

Training vs. Inference

Training is the (costly, offline) process of fitting parameters to data. Inference is the (cheap, online) act of running the finished model to get a prediction — analogous to build time versus runtime.

No. 7

Dataset & Features

The dataset is the collection of examples used for training or evaluation; features are the individual measurable inputs (columns, signals) describing each example.

No. 8

Algorithm vs. Model

The algorithm is the learning procedure itself (e.g. gradient descent); the model is what that procedure produces after being run over data.
