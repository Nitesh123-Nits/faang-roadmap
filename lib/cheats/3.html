A vs B Decision Map

29 common choices: what each solves, when A wins, when B wins, and a default.

INSIDE

29 decisions in 6 groups

A vs B cards with a stated default How to write the choice into an ADR

HOW TO USE

Read this first

Name the problem first.
Check the constraints: team size, scale, consistency need, budget.
Pick the simpler option unless a constraint rules it out.
Write the choice and its revisit trigger in an ADR.
DEFAULTS

Every card ends with a default. Override it with evidence from your constraints, not with taste.

Architecture

ARCHITECTURE

Monolith vs Microservices

Monolith

Microservices

One team, domain still unclear, early product, simple ops.

Independent deploys and scaling, many teams, stable service boundaries.

R U L E Start with a modular monolith; split only when a boundary hurts.

ARCHITECTURE

Orchestration vs Choreography

Orchestration

Choreography

Many steps, need central visibility and compensation logic.

Few steps, loose coupling, teams own their reactions to events.

R U L E Past about 4 steps, choreography becomes hard to trace.

ARCHITECTURE

Event sourcing vs CRUD + audit table

Event sourcing

CRUD + audit

Need full history, temporal queries, replay, rebuildable views.

Current state is enough; team new to the model.

R U L E CRUD is the default; event sourcing is a deliberate cost.

ARCHITECTURE

CQRS vs Single model

CQRS

Single model

Read and write shapes or loads diverge sharply.

Plain CRUD with similar reads and writes.

R U L E Add CQRS for a proven read bottleneck, not up front.

ARCHITECTURE

Serverless vs Containers / K8s

Serverless

Containers / K8s

Spiky or rare load, short tasks, small ops team.

Steady load, long-running work, runtime control, cost at scale.

R U L E Compare cost at your steady-state volume, not at idle.

Data

D ATA

SQL vs NoSQL

SQL

NoSQL

Relations, transactions, adhoc queries, evolving questions.

Known access patterns, huge scale, flexible schema, key-based access.

R U L E Default to SQL until a measured limit says otherwise.

D ATA

Read replicas vs Sharding

Read replicas

Sharding

Read-heavy; one node still holds the writes and data.

Writes or data volume exceed one node.

R U L E Replicas, then caching, then sharding. Sharding is the last step.

D ATA

Leader–follower vs Multi-leader / leaderless

Leader–follower

Multi / leaderless

Single write region, simple conflict-free model.

Multi-region writes or write availability during partitions; conflicts handled.

R U L E Multi-leader means designing conflict resolution.

D ATA

Optimistic vs Pessimistic locking

Optimistic

Pessimistic

Low contention; retries are cheap.

High contention on hot rows; retries are costly.

R U L E Version column first; lock only where conflicts are frequent.

D ATA

DB full-text search vs Search engine

DB full-text

Search engine

Modest data, simple queries, one less system to run.

Relevance ranking, facets, fuzzy match, large corpora.

R U L E Keep search in the DB until relevance or scale demands more.

D ATA

Batch vs Stream

Batch

Stream

Hours of latency is fine; completeness and simplicity matter.

Seconds-level latency; continuous, event-time results.

R U L E Streaming adds state, ordering and replay concerns.

Messaging

MESSAGING

Kafka vs RabbitMQ

Kafka

RabbitMQ

Replayable log, high throughput, many independent consumers, ordering per key.

Task queues, rich routing, per-message ack and retry, priorities.

R U L E Log of facts: Kafka. Queue of jobs: RabbitMQ.

MESSAGING

Kafka vs SQS + SNS

Kafka

SQS + SNS

Replay, stream processing, ordered partitions, high volume.

Managed simplicity, modest volume, no cluster to operate.

R U L E Operating Kafka is a team cost; weigh managed options.

MESSAGING

Sync call vs Async messaging

Sync call

Async messaging

Caller needs the answer now to continue.

Work can finish later; decouple, absorb spikes, isolate failures.

R U L E Async trades latency certainty for resilience.

MESSAGING

Polling vs Push (WS / SSE)

Polling

Push

Infrequent updates, simple clients, tolerate delay.

Frequent real-time updates; many clients watching changes.

R U L E SSE for one-way feeds; WebSocket for two-way.

APIs

A P I S

REST vs gRPC

REST

gRPC

Public APIs, browsers, HTTP caching, wide tooling.

Internal low-latency calls, streaming, strict contracts.

R U L E External: REST. Internal hot paths: consider gRPC.

A P I S

REST vs GraphQL

REST

GraphQL

Resource-shaped data, cacheable, simple clients.

Many client shapes, aggregation across services, over-fetching pain.

R U L E GraphQL moves complexity to caching, auth and N+1.

A P I S

JWT vs Server session

JWT

Server session

Stateless services, crossservice identity, short lifetimes.

Easy revocation, central control, single app.

R U L E Short-lived JWT plus refresh; plan for revocation.

Consistency

CONSISTENCY

Strong vs Eventual consistency

Strong

Eventual

Money, inventory, uniqueness, anything unsafe to be stale.

Feeds, counters, analytics, anything tolerating lag.

R U L E Choose per entity, not per system.

CONSISTENCY

2PC vs Saga

2PC

Saga

Few participants, same trust domain, XA support, short transactions.

Cross-service, longrunning, availability over isolation.

R U L E Saga needs idempotency and compensating actions.

CONSISTENCY

Cache-aside vs Write-through

Cache-aside

Write-through

Read-heavy; stale-until-TTL is acceptable.

Reads must reflect recent writes; accept write latency.

R U L E Write-behind buys write speed at risk of loss.

CONSISTENCY

Redis vs Memcached

Redis

Memcached

Data structures, persistence, pub/sub, replication, clustering.

Plain multi-threaded keyvalue cache and nothing more.

R U L E Redis covers more; Memcached is simpler when sufficient.

Scale & Ops

SCALE & OPS

Vertical vs Horizontal scaling

Vertical

Horizontal

Quick win; stateful or hardto-partition workloads.

Beyond one machine, or HA needed; stateless or partitionable.

R U L E Scale up first if it is cheap; plan the way out.

SCALE & OPS

Active–passive vs Active–active

Active–passive

Active–active

Simpler; failover time is acceptable.

Near-zero downtime, regional load sharing; conflict handling in place.

R U L E Active-active costs data and routing complexity.

SCALE & OPS

Multi-AZ vs Multi-region

Multi-AZ

Multi-region

Default high availability within one region.

Regional-outage RTO, latency to users, data residency.

R U L E Multi-region only when the business case is explicit.

SCALE & OPS

Managed service vs Self-hosted

Managed

Self-hosted

Small team; commodity capability; time matters.

Cost at large scale, deep tuning, compliance or portability needs.

R U L E Run it yourself only if it is core to your edge.

SCALE & OPS

Build vs Buy

Build

Buy

Core differentiator; unique requirements; long horizon.

Commodity capability; speed matters; vendor fits well.

R U L E Buy the commodity, build the differentiator.

SCALE & OPS

Blocking + virtual threads vs Reactive

Virtual threads

Reactive

Simple code, I/O-bound services; readable stack traces.

Streaming pipelines needing backpressure and composition.

R U L E On modern Java, try virtual threads first.

Deploy

DEPLOY
Rolling vs Blue-green vs Canary
Rolling Blue-green Cheap; tolerates Instant rollback; mixed versions needs double briefly. capacity and DB compatibility.Canary Limits blast radius with real traffic; needs metrics and routing.

R U L E Canary for risky changes; rolling for routine ones.

THE RULE

Name the problem, check the constraints, pick the simpler option, and write the revisit trigger into an ADR.
