The eight steps

STEP 1 · 5 MIN

Clarify requirements

DO

Ask for the 3–4 core features, then what is out of scope. Pin down non-functionals: scale, read/write ratio, latency, availability, consistency, retention, regions.

PRODUCE

Written functional and non-functional lists, agreed with the interviewer.

AVOID

Assuming scale. Designing before scope is agreed.

STEP 2 · 4 MIN

Estimate

DO

DAU → average QPS → peak QPS. Storage per year. Bandwidth. Cache size. State every assumption aloud and round hard.

PRODUCE

Four or five numbers that drive later choices (e.g. read QPS decides caching).

AVOID

False precision. Numbers that never influence the design.

STEP 3 · 6 MIN

API and data model

DO

3–5 endpoints: verb, path, key params. Idempotency key on writes. Pagination on lists. Entities, keys and indexes taken from the access patterns. Choose the store per entity.

PRODUCE

Endpoint list and a table or key sketch per entity.

AVOID

A schema with no query behind it.

STEP 4 · 10 MIN

High-level design

DO

Draw the skeleton first, then specialise it. Walk the write path and the read path end to end. Name the store for each entity.

PRODUCE

A box diagram with labelled flows, readable from across the table.

AVOID

Technology names with no stated role. Boxes you cannot justify.

STEP 5 · 12 MIN

Deep dives

DO

Pick the 2–3 hardest parts, or follow the interviewer's lead. For each: the bottleneck, two options, the numbers, the choice, the cost.

PRODUCE

Decisions with reasons, not just components.

AVOID

Skimming five topics instead of owning two.

STEP 6 · 4 MIN

Failure and reliability

DO

Find single points of failure. Replication and failover. Retries with backoff plus idempotency. Dead-letter queue. Degrade gracefully. State RPO and RTO if data matters.

PRODUCE

A short list of failure scenarios and what the user experiences in each.

AVOID

Describing only the happy path.

STEP 7 · 2 MIN

Operations and security

DO

Metrics and SLOs, alerts, tracing. Authentication, authorisation, rate limiting, PII handling. Name the main cost driver.

PRODUCE

Three or four lines, each tied to this system.

AVOID

A generic checklist unrelated to the design.

STEP 8 · 2 MIN

Wrap up

DO

Restate the key decisions and their trade-offs. What breaks at 10× scale, and what you would build next.

PRODUCE

A confident 30-second summary.

AVOID

Trailing off. Introducing new components at the end.

REFERENCE

Estimation recipe

requests/day ÷ 86,400 = avg QPS
peak ≈ 2–3 × avg  (assumed)
storage/yr = writes/day × size × 365
            × replication factor (often 3)
bandwidth = QPS × payload size
in-flight = QPS × latency  (Little's Law)
cache ≈ hot set  (rule of thumb ~20%)

1M/day ≈ 12 QPS
100M/day ≈ 1.2K QPS
REFERENCE

Numbers to remember

99.9%≈ 8.8 hours of downtime / year
99.99%≈ 53 minutes / year
99.999%≈ 5 minutes / year
Memory read~100 ns
SSD random read~100 µs
Same-DC round trip~0.5 ms
Cross-continent~100–150 ms

Orders of magnitude only; hardware and networks vary.

SAY IT LIKE THIS

The trade-off sentence

TEMPLATE

“I’d choose X because the requirement is Y. The cost is Z. I’d revisit if W changes.”

Offer two options before choosing one.
Tie each choice to a number or a requirement.
Name the simpler alternative and why it falls short.

EVALUATION

What interviewers look for

Scoping and clarifying questions
Structure and pacing
Numbers that drive decisions
Trade-off reasoning and depth in a few areas
Failure thinking
Handling pushback without defensiveness
MISTAKES

Common failures

Drawing boxes before requirements
No estimation, or numbers that are ignored
A buzzword stack with no justification
A monologue with no check-ins
Over-engineering for 10× on day one
Contradicting your own earlier numbers

Requirements drive numbers, numbers drive components, and every component earns its place with a reason and a stated cost.
