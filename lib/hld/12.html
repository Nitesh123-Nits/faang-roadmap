Distributed Job Scheduler

Cron at scale

Functional Requirements
Schedule one-time and recurring (cron) jobs
Trigger jobs at the right time
Execute across a pool of workers
Retry with backoff; dead-letter after max attempts
Priorities and per-tenant concurrency limits
Pause, resume, cancel and update jobs
Execution history, logs and alerts
Non-Functional Requirements
Reliability: no missed triggers, no duplicate runs
Accuracy: trigger drift under 1 second
Scalability: 10M jobs, 12K triggers/s at peak
HA: scheduler failover in seconds (leases)
Execution: at-least-once, jobs must be idempotent
Fault tolerance: worker crash reassigns the job
Observability: lag, failures and queue depth

Key design trade-offs

At-least-once + idempotent jobs vs exactly-once

Partition by time bucket (no double fire) vs single leader

Push to workers vs workers pull from queue

Create Job

Client → Gateway → Job Service → Job Store (stores next_run_at)

Trigger

Scheduler polls due jobs for its partitions → enqueues to Work Queue → Workers pull and run

Result

Worker writes status to Execution Store; failures retry with backoff, then dead-letter + alert

Job Store is partitioned by time bucket; each Scheduler instance owns a set of buckets, so no job fires twice.

After max retries the job moves to a dead-letter topic and an alert is raised.

Scaling & Failure Handling R

Scheduler failover: partition leases expire in seconds; another node takes over the buckets
Worker crash: missed heartbeat makes the Reaper re-queue the job; jobs must be idempotent
Spikes: top-of-hour bursts absorbed by Kafka; workers autoscale on consumer lag
Poison jobs: capped retries with backoff, then dead-letter topic and alert
