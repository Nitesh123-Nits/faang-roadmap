PAGE SPLITS IN A CLUSTERED INDEX

A B+ tree has leaves 10, 20, 30, 40 | 60, 70, 80, 90. Inserting 25, 35 and 45 into the middle of full pages splits them, creating new pages and more of them overall: frag‐ mentation increases.

IMPACT
More disk I/O, since data is scattered.
Slower queries: range scans and joins suffer.
More space used, as internal fragmentation wastes it.
Lower throughput: more I/O means lower performance.
HOW TO REDUCE IT
Use sequential inserts (identity / auto‐increment keys).
Avoid random inserts into clustered indexes. Random GUIDs cause high page

splits.

Use a fill factor: leave some free space in pages.
Use an appropriate page size.
Maintain regularly: rebuild or reorganise indexes, defragment tables (DBMS‐spe‐

cific).

Partition large tables.
MEMORY TRICK

A bookshelf is a set of pages. To insert a book in the middle, you create a new shelf and move some books onto it. That is a page split, and it is what causes fragmenta‐ tion.

R E M E M B E R . Fragmentation happens when data is not stored contiguously on

disk, causing more I/O, slower queries and wasted space. Main causes: inserts, updates, deletes. Prevent with good design and regular maintenance.
