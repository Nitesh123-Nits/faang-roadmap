BAD DESIGN VERSUS GOOD DESIGN

A single orders table that repeats user_name , user_email and user_phone on every row stores the same user data many times. Change an email and you must change every row; miss one and the data is inconsistent.

The fix: a users table ( id , name , email , created_at ) and an orders table that holds only user_id as a foreign key plus order-specific columns.

DENORMALIZATION: WHEN AND HOW

Denormalise deliberately, once you have measured a problem.

WHENHOW
Read performance is criticalAdd redundant data
Complex joins hurt performancePre-compute aggregates
The data is relatively stableUse materialised views

Example: instead of summing order_items every time you show an order, store total_amount on orders . You balance write cost (keep it in sync) against read cost.

R E M E M B E R . Normalise first for correctness; denormalise selectively for read

speed. Never the other way round.
