NOT EQUAL / NOT IN

-> , NOT IN and -= have low selectivity and are usually inefficient for indexes. The

database may prefer a full scan.

LOW SELECTIVITY

When 90% of rows are ACTIVE , an index on status is not selective, and the data‐ base rightly prefers scanning over index lookups.

COMPOSITE INDEX RULE VIOLATION

An index on (A, B, C) works only if queries use the leftmost columns.

WHERE b = 1                -- skips A: index not usable
WHERE a = 1 AND b = 1      -- usable
WRAPPING IN A SUBQUERY OR VIEW

The optimizer may not push predicates down, leading to full scans.

WHERE id IN (SELECT id FROM t WHERE status = 'A')
OUTDATED STATISTICS

Poor or outdated statistics mislead the optimizer: a wrong plan is chosen and the index is ignored. Typical cause: a table updated heavily after a bulk insert or up‐ date. Refresh with ANALYZE .

BONUS

Sometimes the query is just not selective enough, or the table is too small for an in‐ dex to matter.

R E M E M B E R . Indexes help the right query. Write SQL the optimizer can love.

Good schema + good data + good SQL = happy indexes. Measure, analyse, op‐ timise, repeat.
