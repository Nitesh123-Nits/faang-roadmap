SQL Joins

Joins combine rows from two or more tables based on a related column.

Assume two tables. A (Employees): (1, John, 10), (2, Mary, 20), (3, Mike, 30), (4, Sara, 40). B (Departments): (10, HR), (20, IT), (30, Finance), (50, Sales). They match on DeptID ; Sara's de‐ partment 40 has no match in B, and department 50 has no employee in A.

SYNTAX
SELECT * FROM A INNER JOIN B ON A.DeptID = B.DeptID;
SELECT * FROM A LEFT  JOIN B ON A.DeptID = B.DeptID;
SELECT * FROM A RIGHT JOIN B ON A.DeptID = B.DeptID;
SELECT * FROM A FULL OUTER JOIN B ON A.DeptID = B.DeptID;
SELECT * FROM A CROSS JOIN B;

-- Self join: employee and manager
SELECT e1.Name AS Employee, e2.Name AS Manager
FROM Employees e1
LEFT JOIN Employees e2 ON e1.ManagerID = e2.ID;

MySQL has no FULL OUTER JOIN ; emulate it with LEFT JOIN --. UNION --. RIGHT JOIN .

WHICH ONE TO USE
INNER: you need only related data.
LEFT: keep all records from the left table, with or without a match.
RIGHT: keep all records from the right table.
FULL OUTER: you need all data from both tables.
CROSS: rarely needed in real scenarios. Be sure you meant it.

R E M E M B E R . A filter on the right table in WHERE turns a LEFT JOIN into an inner

join. Put such conditions in the ON clause to keep the unmatched left rows.
