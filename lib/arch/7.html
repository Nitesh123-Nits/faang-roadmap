Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA)

WHAT IS IT?
FUNDAMENTAL IDEA

Enterprise capabilities exposed as reusable, contractbased services, typically orchestrated through an Enterprise Service Bus (ESB).

Compose business processes from shared services with standardized contracts (SOAP/WSDL, XML) mediated by middleware.

PROBLEM IT SOLVES
WHY IT WAS INTRODUCED

Siloed applications and point-to-point integrations in large enterprises.

Introduced in the 2000s to reuse business capabilities across departments and integrate heterogeneous systems.

How it works

A channel calls an enterprise service through the ESB.
The ESB looks up the service contract and routes the message.
It transforms protocols and payload formats where needed.
Composite processes are orchestrated centrally (BPEL-style).
Services access shared enterprise data stores.
Governance enforces schemas, versions and policies.
SERVICE-ORIENTED ARCHITECTURE (SOA)

Advantages

Disadvantages

Reuse of coarse-grained business capabilities.
The ESB becomes a smart, monolithic bottleneck.

+ Integrates legacy and heterogeneous platforms.

Heavy XML/SOAP contracts and tooling.
Strong contracts and central governance.
Shared databases couple services at the data layer.
Central security and monitoring at the bus.
Slow change cycles due to central governance.

When to use

When not to use

Large enterprises integrating many legacy systems under strict governance and standard contracts.

Fast-moving product teams that need independent deployment (prefer microservices); greenfield cloudnative systems.
