Synchronous vs Asynchronous Communication

Synchronous calls block the caller until a response arrives; asynchronous communication decouples sender and receiver through messages and callbacks. Most systems need both.

Complexity trade-offs

Suitable use cases

Synchronous design is simple until dependency chains lengthen; asynchronous design is complex from the start but degrades gracefully. The usual compromise is synchronous at the edge for queries and asynchronous between services for state changes.

Synchronous: queries and user-facing operations needing immediate results.
Asynchronous: workflows, integration, background work, fan-out.

Practical recommendation. Use synchronous request/response for queries and short user-facing commands with timeouts and circuit breakers. Use asynchronous events or queues for cross-service state changes and long-running work.
