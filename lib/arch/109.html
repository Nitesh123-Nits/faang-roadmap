Actor-Based Architecture

WHAT IS IT?
FUNDAMENTAL IDEA

Computation is organized as independent actors that own private state and communicate only by asynchronous messages.

Share nothing, send messages: each actor processes one message at a time, eliminating locks.

PROBLEM IT SOLVES
WHY IT WAS INTRODUCED

Shared-memory concurrency bugs and difficulty scaling and distributing stateful logic.

Hewitt’s Actor model, realized in Erlang and Akka, enabled massively concurrent and fault-tolerant systems.

How it works

Each actor has private state and a mailbox.
Actors send immutable messages asynchronously.
An actor processes one message at a time, so no locks are needed.
An actor can create child actors and change its behavior.
Supervisors restart failed children (“let it crash”).
Actors can be located on remote nodes transparently.

Disadvantages

+ Natural fault isolation and supervision.

Message-passing complexity and harder debugging.

+ Scales across cores and machines.

Mailbox overload and backpressure need design.

+ Good fit for stateful, entity-oriented workloads.

When to use

When not to use

Stateful entities at scale (devices, sessions, games), telecom, trading and highly concurrent systems.

Simple stateless CRUD services and CPU-bound batch work.
