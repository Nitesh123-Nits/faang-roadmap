VIII.01

Rule-Based Architecture

WHAT IS IT?
FUNDAMENTAL IDEA

Behavior is expressed as declarative business rules evaluated by a rule engine against facts, rather than hard-coded in procedural logic.

Separate the frequently changing “what to do when” from the stable application code so rules can change without redeployment.

PROBLEM IT SOLVES
WHY IT WAS INTRODUCED

Complex, volatile decision logic scattered across code that business users cannot review or change.

Expert systems and business rule management systems (Drools, ILOG) made rules first-class artifacts.

How it works

The application inserts facts into working memory.
The engine matches facts against rule conditions.
Matching rules form a conflict set.
Conflict resolution chooses which rule fires.
Actions update facts or trigger side effects, possibly firing further rules.
The final decisions are returned to the application.
RULE-BASED ARCHITECTURE

Advantages

Disadvantages

Business-readable, changeable rules without redeployment.
Hard to debug rule interactions and ordering.
Rule bases can become unmanageable.

When not to use
