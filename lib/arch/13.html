Peer-to-Peer Architecture

WHAT IS IT?
FUNDAMENTAL IDEA

A network of equal nodes that each act as both client and server, sharing resources directly without a central authority.

Distribute load and responsibility across participants so capacity grows with the number of peers.

PROBLEM IT SOLVES
WHY IT WAS INTRODUCED

Central bottlenecks and single points of failure and control in client-server systems.

Introduced for file sharing and later underpins blockchains and decentralized protocols.

Peers connect directly; a tracker helps discovery only

How it works

A new peer contacts a bootstrap node or DHT to discover others.
It connects to several peers and advertises what it holds.
Requests are routed directly between peers.
Data is replicated across peers for availability.
Peers join and leave freely; the overlay self-heals.
Integrity is verified by hashes or consensus rather than trusted servers.
PEER-TO-PEER ARCHITECTURE

Advantages

Disadvantages

No central point of failure; resilient to node loss.

+ Capacity scales with participants.

Hard to guarantee consistency, security and quality of service.

+ Lower infrastructure cost for the provider.

Discovery, NAT traversal and churn add complexity.

+ Censorship-resistant designs are possible.

Difficult governance and updates.
Malicious peers must be handled.

When to use

When not to use

Content distribution, decentralized ledgers, collaboration with CRDTs, and resilient mesh or edge scenarios.

Systems needing strong consistency, central authorization or predictable latency.
