Serverless vs Container-Based Architecture

Both avoid managing physical servers. Serverless abstracts runtime and scaling entirely and bills per execution; containers give you control over the runtime and typically bill for provisioned capacity.

Complexity trade-offs

Suitable use cases

Serverless trades control for operational simplicity; the break-even point is sustained throughput, where always-on containers become cheaper. Debugging and local testing favor containers.

Serverless: spiky, event-driven, low-traffic or glue workloads.
Containers: steady traffic, long-running processes, custom runtimes, portability needs.

Practical recommendation. Default to containers for core services with steady load and use serverless for event handlers and bursty edges. Avoid committing to either dogmatically; mix them behind stable interfaces.
