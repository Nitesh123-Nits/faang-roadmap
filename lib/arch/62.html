Strangler Fig Architecture

WHAT IS IT?
FUNDAMENTAL IDEA

Incrementally replace a legacy system by routing functionality to new services behind a facade until the old system can be retired.

Grow the new system around the old one, one slice at a time, instead of a risky big-bang rewrite.

PROBLEM IT SOLVES
WHY IT WAS INTRODUCED

High-risk rewrites of legacy systems that cannot be paused or replaced all at once.

Martin Fowler named it after strangler figs that gradually envelop host trees.

How it works

Place a facade or proxy in front of the legacy system.
Pick one capability and build it as a new service.
Route that capability’s traffic to the new service.
Synchronize or migrate the relevant data.
Repeat until the legacy system has no remaining responsibilities.
Decommission the legacy system.

Disadvantages

Incremental, low-risk migration.

+ Value delivered early and continuously.

Long-running dual systems and data synchronization.

+ Easy rollback by changing routes.

Facade can become a bottleneck.

+ Legacy keeps running during the transition.

Needs careful seam identification.
Risk of never finishing the migration.

When to use

When not to use

Replacing or modernizing legacy monoliths while they remain in production.

Small systems where a rewrite is cheaper, or when no clean seams exist.
