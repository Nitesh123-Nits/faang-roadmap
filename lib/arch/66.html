Fan-Out / Fan-In Architecture

WHAT IS IT?
FUNDAMENTAL IDEA

Work is split into many parallel tasks (fan-out) and the results are collected and combined (fan-in).

Parallelize independent work to cut latency or increase throughput, then aggregate.

PROBLEM IT SOLVES
WHY IT WAS INTRODUCED

Long-running sequential processing of independent units of work.

Elastic compute makes parallel decomposition cheap; MapReduce popularized the idea.

Splitter distributes tasks; aggregator joins results

How it works

A coordinator splits a job into independent tasks.
Tasks are placed on a queue or invoked in parallel.
Workers process tasks concurrently and report results.
An aggregator collects results and tracks completion.
Failed or slow tasks are retried or timed out.
The merged result is stored or returned.

Disadvantages

When to use

When not to use

Batch jobs, document or image processing, scattergather queries, and map/reduce style computation.

Strictly sequential dependent steps or tiny tasks where overhead dominates.
