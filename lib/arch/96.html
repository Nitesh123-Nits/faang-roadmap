VII.08

Edge Computing Architecture

WHAT IS IT?
FUNDAMENTAL IDEA

Processing is moved close to data sources and users

on devices, gateways or edge nodes — rather than in a distant central cloud.

Compute where data is created to reduce latency, bandwidth cost and dependence on connectivity.

PROBLEM IT SOLVES
WHY IT WAS INTRODUCED

Latency, bandwidth, privacy and offline needs for IoT, video and real-time applications.

Billions of devices generate data that is costly or too slow to ship to central data centers.

How it works

Devices send raw data to a nearby edge gateway.
The gateway filters and aggregates data and runs local inference.
Time-critical decisions return to devices without a cloud round trip.
Summaries and exceptions are forwarded to regional and cloud tiers.
The cloud trains models and manages fleets.
Updated models and configuration are pushed back to the edge.
EDGE COMPUTING ARCHITECTURE

Advantages

Very low latency and local autonomy.

+ Reduced bandwidth and cloud costs.

Works through intermittent connectivity.
Keeps sensitive data local.

When to use

IoT, industrial automation, video analytics, AR/VR and latency-sensitive services.

Real-world applications

Factory predictive maintenance
Retail video analytics
Connected vehicles
CDN edge functions.

Quick summary

Disadvantages

Distributed fleet management and updates are hard.
Limited compute and storage at the edge.
Security surface across many devices.
Data consistency between tiers.

When not to use

Workloads without latency, bandwidth or privacy needs where central cloud suffices.

Key engineering considerations

Design for offline-first and store-and-forward. Automate secure OTA updates. Define what runs at each tier. Monitor device health centrally.
