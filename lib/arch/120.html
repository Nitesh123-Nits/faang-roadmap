Lambda vs Kappa Architecture

Both serve real-time and historical analytics. Lambda runs a batch layer and a speed layer in parallel and merges them; Kappa uses one stream pipeline and handles reprocessing by replaying the log.

Complexity trade-offs

Suitable use cases

Lambda duplicates business logic and operations; Kappa concentrates complexity in the streaming engine and log retention. Modern stream engines and lakehouse streaming have made Kappa-style designs more viable.

Lambda: when complex historical computation cannot be done in the stream engine.
Kappa: event-centric systems where streaming can deliver correct results.

Practical recommendation. Prefer Kappa-style single-pipeline designs on a durable log. Keep a batch path only where historical computation genuinely cannot be expressed in the stream engine.
