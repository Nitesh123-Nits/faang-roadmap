Quorum overlap. With W + R > N, every read quorum intersects every write quorum — necessary but not sufficient for strong consistency.

Synchronous follower: commit waits for it →no data loss on failover, higher latency, blocks if the follower is down. Semi‐

sync : wait for one of N.

Failover hazards: promoting a lagging follower loses acknowledged writes; old leader comes back and still thinks it's leader

(split‐brain) →need fencing (STONITH, epochs, tokens).

5.7Consensus Basics: Raft and Leader Election

Problem: get nodes to agree on a sequence of values despite crashes. Used for leader election, configuration, locks,

metadata (etcd →Kubernetes, ZooKeeper, KRaft in Kafka).

Raft in four lines: nodes are follower/candidate/leader; a follower that hears no heartbeat becomes candidate, increments

the term, requests votes; a majority makes it leader; the leader appends entries and commits once a majority has them.

Tolerates ⌊(N−1)/2⌋failures: 3 nodes →1, 5 nodes →2. Even sizes add cost without extra tolerance.

Majority requirement means a minority partition cannot make progress (that's the "C" choice). Latency: every commit needs a majority round‐trip; cross‐region clusters pay WAN latency on each write.
