Final clean-code checklist

Does the class name describe its responsibility?
Does the method name explain its intent?
Are variable names meaningful?
Are boolean names expressed as questions?
Are abbreviations understandable?
Are naming conventions consistent?
Are methods focused on one logical task?
Does each class have a cohesive responsibility?
Are magic numbers avoided where appropriate?
Do comments explain why rather than what?
Are configuration values externalised appropriately?
Are duplication and unnecessary abstractions avoided?
Can another developer understand it without asking its author?The one principle to remember. Write code for the developer who will maintain it six months from now, not just for the compiler that executes it today.
