Dependency Injection and IoC

DI is a design principle, not a framework feature. Dependencies (objects a class needs) are provided from the outside, rather than created inside the class. The container injects them for you.

Goal: loose coupling, high cohesion, easy testing, better maintainability.

WHY DI

Loose coupling, easy unit testing (mocking), reusability, maintainability, scalability, and alignment with SOLID principles, especially Inversion of Control.

INVERSION OF CONTROL

WITHOUT DI (YOU CONTROL)WITH DI (THE CONTAINER CONTROLS)
UserRepository repo = new UserRepository();@Autowired UserService service;
Tight couplingLoose coupling

In traditional programming you create objects and control the flow. With DI, control is inverted: the container creates objects and manages their lifecycle.
