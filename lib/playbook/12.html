DEEP DIVE

Meta's caches sit in front of a huge database tier and are kept in sync by invalidation events flowing from the database. Races between concurrent writes, reads and invalidations can still leave a cache entry stale. Rather than assume perfection, Meta built a service to observe consistency.

Polaris

It consumes the same invalidation stream and, for each event, asks cache servers whether they now hold a stale value.
To avoid hammering the database, it re-checks mismatches at growing intervals (minutes) before declaring a real inconsistency. Most apparent mismatches are just inflight events that resolve themselves.
It treats a cache as a client of the database and defines consistency as “eventually reflects the latest write”, then reports a fraction of writes that stay inconsistent beyond a window.

Finding the cause Polaris says that something is stale; a tracing library embedded in cache servers explains why. It records only the narrow time window in which a race could occur, for the affected keys, so the volume stays manageable. Engineers can then see the interleaving of events that caused the bug.

TA K E AWAY

Two ideas transfer to any system: define consistency as a measurable SLO, and pair a cheap detector with a targeted tracer so you can both notice and debug rare races. In your own services, a periodic reconciliation job that compares cache and DB samples is a small version of the same thing.
