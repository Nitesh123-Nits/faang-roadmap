DEEP DIVE

“Eleven nines” (99.999999999%) is a durability target, meaning the chance of losing a given object in a year is vanishingly small. It is not magic; it comes from layering several techniques and operating them with discipline.

Erasure coding Instead of storing three full copies (200% overhead), data is split into k data shards and m parity shards computed with Reed-Solomon style codes. Any k of the k+m shards can rebuild the object. You survive several simultaneous losses at far lower storage cost than replication.

The supporting cast

Failure domains. Shards land on different disks, racks and facilities with independent power and networking.
Checksums end to end. Verified on upload, on every stored shard and again on read, so silent corruption (bit rot) is caught. Sending the checksum as an HTTP trailer lets large streams be verified without buffering.
Verify before acknowledging. A write is confirmed only after the stored shards are checked.
Fast, continuous repair. Durability depends on how quickly lost shards are rebuilt, because the risk window is the time spent degraded. Spare capacity on every disk keeps repair throughput high, and repair capacity is scaled from observed failure rates.
Human safeguards. Versioning, backups and a durability review for risky changes.
TA K E AWAY

Durability = independent failure domains × short repair window × constant verification.
