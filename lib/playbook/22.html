Retries, Timeouts, Circuit Breakers and Rate Limits

THE IDEA

The four defences that stop one slow dependency from taking everything down.

HOW IT WORKS
1

Set timeout Every call gets a deadline

2

Retry with backoff Few attempts, jittered delay

3

Open circuit if failing Fail fast when unhealthy

4

Fallback / shed load Degrade gracefully

5

Rate limit callers Cap what callers may send

REMEMBER
Timeout everything; retry only idempotent calls.
Backoff with jitter prevents retry storms.
Bulkheads isolate failures.
DEEP DIVE

Timeouts

Every network call needs a timeout. Without one, a slow dependency ties up threads until your own service is the one that fails. Set a budget per request and propagate a deadline downward so inner calls never outlive the outer one.

Retries

Retry only idempotent operations and only transient errors (timeouts, 503, 429), never 4xx validation errors.
Use exponential backoff with jitter: sleep = random(0, min(cap, base × 2^attempt)) . Jitter spreads retries so clients do not all return in lockstep.
Cap attempts and total time. Retries at several layers multiply (3 × 3 × 3 = 27 calls), so retry at one layer.

Circuit breaker

Tracks failures; when the failure rate crosses a threshold it opens and fails fast for a cooldown period, then half-opens to probe with a few trial calls. This gives the dependency room to recover and gives callers a fast, predictable error.

Bulkheads and load shedding Isolate resources per dependency (separate thread pools or semaphores) so one failing service cannot exhaust the pool. Under overload, reject low-priority work early rather than slowing everything down.

TRADE-OFF

Aggressive retries during an outage create a retry storm that prevents recovery. Prefer a retry budget (e.g. at most 10% extra traffic) over a fixed count.
