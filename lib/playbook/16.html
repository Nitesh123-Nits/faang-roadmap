DEEP DIVE

Micro frontends apply the microservice idea to the browser. Instead of one large singlepage app owned by many teams, the UI is composed from vertical slices that each own a business capability end to end: UI, backend-for-frontend and data.

Rules that keep it sane

Split by business domain, not by technical layer.
Communicate through events and URLs, not shared global state.
Share a design system, not business code, to keep the look consistent.
Pair each slice with a backend-for-frontend so its API fits its UI.
Agree on runtime contracts and test them (consumer-driven contracts).
HONEST TRADE-OFF

Micro frontends add bundle size, duplicated dependencies and operational overhead. They pay off with many autonomous teams and a mature platform. For a team of ten, a modular monolith frontend is usually the better design.
