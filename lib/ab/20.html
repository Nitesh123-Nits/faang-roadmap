Database and Data Modeling

Part II covers the decisions that fix your system’s correctness and capacity ceilings: which kind of database holds the data, how it is modeled, how it scales, how copies stay in step, and how services share (or do not share) it. Data decisions are the hardest to undo, so interviewers probe them for reasoning about access patterns, invariants, and failure.

The eighteen comparisons form five questions. Model (15–18) asks what shape the data takes. Workload and layout (19–20, 28–29) asks how reads, writes, and history are organized. Scale and copies (21–24) asks how data grows and replicates. Correctness (25–27) asks which guarantees you keep. Data across services (30–32) asks who owns data and how changes propagate.

The running example continues: an e-commerce platform with orders, payments, and catalog data on PostgreSQL, Redis, and a document store, with Kafka carrying changes.

Questions interviewers ask in this domain

Why would you pick PostgreSQL over a document database for orders, and when would you reverse that?
Your orders table is slow and growing fast. Walk through indexing, partitioning, replicas, and sharding in order.
Two users buy the last item simultaneously. How do you prevent overselling, and what does each locking choice cost?
What does replication lag break, and how do you design around it?
How do you keep Elasticsearch or a cache in sync with the primary database without dual writes?

How would you paginate a feed with millions of items and constant inserts?
