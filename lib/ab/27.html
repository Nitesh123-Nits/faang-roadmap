Vertical Scaling vs Horizontal Scaling

Make one machine bigger, or add more machines, and see what each does to complexity, cost, and failure.

THE 30-SECOND ANSWER

Vertical scaling adds CPU, memory, or I/O to one node: no application changes, but a hardware ceiling and a single failure domain. Horizontal scaling adds nodes behind a balancer or partitions data across them: more capacity and resilience, but you take on distribution, state, and consistency. Scale vertically first when it is cheap and simple; go horizontal for stateless tiers early, and for stateful tiers when limits or availability demand it.

When should you choose vertical scaling vs horizontal scaling?
SCALE VERTICALLYYou are well below hardware limits, need strong consistency, and want the least complexity.
SCALE HORIZONTALLYYou need elasticity or fault tolerance, or you are near the largest practical node.
HYBRIDHorizontal for stateless tiers; vertical plus replicas for the primary database, with sharding when needed.
