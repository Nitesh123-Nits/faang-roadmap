ACID vs BASE

Compare the formal transaction guarantees of relational databases with the availability-first style of many distributed stores.

THE 30-SECOND ANSWER

ACID (atomicity, consistency, isolation, durability) defines what a transaction guarantees, and relational databases implement it with defined isolation levels. BASE (basically available, soft state, eventual consistency) is a loose description of systems that favor availability and scale over immediate consistency; unlike ACID, it is a design stance rather than a precise specification. Use ACID where invariants matter and BASE-style designs where scale or availability dominate, with compensations for the gaps.
